# Liquid Glass: research notes (2026-11-18)

- **What Apple says it is.** An optical glass layer for floating controls. It reflects and refracts what is under it, adapts to the content beneath (light or dark) so controls stay legible, takes a judicious tint, and morphs and merges shapes inside a GlassEffectContainer. Glass variants: `regular` (frosted and adaptive, the default), `clear` (more see-through, needs a dimming layer for legibility), `identity` (no effect), plus `.tint(color)` and `.interactive()`. Reduce Transparency turns it into a solid. Sources: [Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass), [Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glass), [Landmarks tutorial](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/landmarks-building-an-app-with-liquid-glass).
- **Lens profile (kube.io).** Treat the shape as a slab with a convex bevel band at the rim. The surface height across the band is the squircle `y = (1 - (1 - x)^4)^(1/4)`, where x is 0 at the outer edge and 1 where the flat interior begins. Bend a vertical ray with Snell's law (n = 1.5) through the slope. Displacement is perpendicular to the edge, near zero on the flat top, and peaks close to the outer rim. It is computed once per distance and swept around the shape. [kube.io: Liquid Glass in CSS/SVG](https://kube.io/blog/liquid-glass-css-svg/)
- **Displacement map and chromatic split.** Rasterise the shape's SDF into an X/Y displacement map (neutral grey = no shift), then sample R, G and B with slightly different scales to get chromatic aberration at the rim. [rdev/liquid-glass-react](https://github.com/rdev/liquid-glass-react) uses scale 70, blur 0.0625, saturation 140%, aberration 2. [rizroze/liquid-glass](https://github.com/rizroze/liquid-glass) uses per-channel offsets [0, 10, 20]. [MarcosDemik/liquidglass](https://github.com/MarcosDemik/liquidglass) builds a GLSL SDF of a rounded rectangle and uses the same per-channel scheme. [shuding/liquid-glass](https://github.com/shuding/liquid-glass) uses a rounded-rect SDF with a smoothstep edge in a fragment shader.
- **Light.** The specular rim brightness is proportional to dot(normal, light direction) with a fixed light angle (kube.io uses -60°, opacity 0.2-0.5). The far rim gets a fainter highlight. A soft inner shadow sits just inside the edge.
- **Frost and tint.** A light blur (a few px), a slight saturation boost (about 120-140%), and a very light tint or wash (white over dark content, faint dark over bright content).
- **Legibility.** Pick light or dark content from the backdrop luminance, with hysteresis so it doesn't flicker. Add a faint backing (a 'clear'-variant dimming layer) when contrast is marginal.

## What WorldClock does (glass.py)
- **Shape and distance.** Mask the shape and take an SDF: analytic for the settled capsule, and cv2.distanceTransform on the rasterised QPainterPath while the shape is animating.
- **Refraction.** A squircle lens profile across a 14 px bevel pulls the backdrop inward (magnifies), with a flat centre.
- **Rim effects.** Per-channel remap gives the dispersion. A Gaussian frost follows.
- **Lighting and content colour.** Specular highlight from the normal against a light at top-left. Inner shadow, tint and luminance-adaptive content colour.
